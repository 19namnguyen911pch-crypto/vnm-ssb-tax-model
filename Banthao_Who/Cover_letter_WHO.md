Dear Editors,

We submit for consideration as a **Policy & Practice** article: **"The excise base, not the headline rate, drives the health gain from Viet Nam's sugar-sweetened beverage tax: a modelling study."**

Viet Nam legislated its first excise tax on sugar-sweetened beverages in June 2025 and provides for review in 2028. Almost all of the preceding debate concerned the rate. Our study shows that the rate is not the binding constraint: because the excise is levied on an ex-factory base and cascaded through value-added tax, a 10% statutory rate produces a retail price increase of about 5%, roughly a quarter of the 20% benchmark WHO recommends. The health gain scales with that price change, not with the rate.

**What was already known.** Excise taxes on sugar-sweetened beverages reduce consumption, and WHO recommends that they raise retail prices by at least 20%. Viet Nam legislated its first such tax in June 2025, but no analysis had estimated what the enacted design would deliver on its own statutory base.

**What this study adds.** The enacted rates correspond to a retail price increase of about 5%, and the resulting health gain — 16 898 disability-adjusted life years averted over 2027–2050 — is small in proportion; applying the same 10% rate to the retail price would nearly double it. Financing is regressively distributed, though by a small margin: the tax absorbs 0.13% of household expenditure in the poorest quintile against 0.08% in the richest. The distribution of the health gain depends on two gradients that have not been measured in Viet Nam, disease risk by expenditure and the quintile gradient in price response; we report the resulting range rather than a point estimate. The paper therefore speaks directly to the 2028 review and to other middle-income countries designing similar taxes.

The study models the tax as legislated — its statutory base, its threshold as operationalised in the national standard, and the Vietnamese value-added tax treatment — rather than a hypothetical design. It places health benefit and tax payment on the same expenditure-quintile scale, and it states which part of the distributional result rests on assumption.

The manuscript has 2 941 words in the body, an abstract of 249 words and 27 references, within the limits for Policy & Practice articles. It uses only published parameters and secondary data, involved no participants, and required no ethical approval. Both authors are nationals of Viet Nam and affiliated with a Vietnamese institution. The analytic code, parameter tables and probabilistic analysis are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23852908. The study received no specific funding and we declare no competing interests.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, has not been published, and both authors have approved the submission.
